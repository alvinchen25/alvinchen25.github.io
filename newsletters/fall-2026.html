In Hong Kong there isn't a Fall, not really. It's still humid and only a little less hot, and the trees here are evergreen. Growing up on the east coast, fall is the best season; you can feel the freshness in the food and the air, you can go to the state fair and see people show off their pumpkins, and if you're lucky you'll drive up to the mountains and see that they too are orange all over, a long summer's setting sun.

Fall has always meant newness—a new school year or a new job, and this year, a new country. Some recent updates!

  • I went to London in late september and fall was in full swing. London was all in all a bit posh for me, too dressed and well-mannered—but I liked the Thames, all its footbridges and riverside parks.
  • The next week, I was in Swiss alps with Tiger, and we hiked from town to town. Switzerland is a place for lovers, we discovered; in one hotel there were flower petals everywhere and a couples bathtub hahaha.
  • October, i was back in HK and I went to this thing called a 'cyclothon'. Hong Kong is very serious about these sorts of public activities.
    • It's a whole mess; I forget my bib at home, my bike has a flat tire near the start line, i hurry back to the bike rental and find that it is left with only broken artifacts, of which I choose the one that looks the least damaged.
    • But things go okay! The course is tough, lots of tunnels and bridges, but I think I finally know how to ride a proper bike with gears, and there's a real reward in the sunrise that day, and taking it in together with so many other people.
      • Apparently the sun looks redder in Hong Kong, because there are more particles in the air to disperse the light.
  • I've been to shenzhen a few times. It's a common weekend activity here, because you can just take the subway over, and most anything you can buy is cheaper in shenzhen.
    • I find myself at karaoke and hotpot (two classic shenzhen activities) with a group of taiwanese thirty-somethings, after meeting one at the cyclothon. The journey finding friends in a new place has taken me to interesting places :).
    • Another time, I go to the spa with some work-friends. 'spa' is an overloaded term but in china it means something very specific. It's an all-inclusive resort, but without the beach part (can't have the sun exposure), so it feels like you're inside a multi-story wooden box—there are massage floors, arcade floors, food floors; loads and loads of consumption and relaxation. Between the spa and the outside world is a mandatory shower + change into spa clothes, and once inside you see people are snacking on the infinite fruit and scrolling reels on big beanbags.
  • Space is more precious in Hong Kong than anywhere in the world; there is only so much usable land around the mountains. I thought new york was crowded, but everything is still bigger in America.
    • In local restaurants, you often share tables with strangers. Parties of two or three are combined around traditional round tables, and sometimes the food gets shared as well. Sometimes you sit so close to other people that it would be awkward to not talk to them, and pour them some tea.
  • I have been reading lots of history lately. some nuggets
    • Hong Kong was, in some sense, built on oysters.
      • Because oysters can be baked to make lime, and the lime is then mixed into concrete.
      • another place with lots of native oysters is the outer banks. there I remember old buildings covered in them, too.
      • I used to think of going to the ocean as a whole event, involving a day of driving, small fishing towns, and saltiness that carries far inland, so you can smell the sea long before you see it. But here in Hong Kong, the water takes you by surprise. It's all around you, literally, because the toilets use ocean water.
    • 1920s Singapore had a population of around 400k, and there were around thirty thousand rickshaw pullers.
      • one thing about living in Asia, is that it's really clear that service and labor are simply much cheaper than in the US or Europe, and there is a clear class divide between the people who provide the services—most commonly these days, domestic helpers—and those who use them.
        • When touring apartments in Hong Kong, I saw many apartments with a maid's room, which often had no windows.
      • It feels like much of the strife in this part of the world comes from colonial-era racial tensions trying to be resolved in post-colonial times
    • I read about this guy named Anwar Ibrahim, who was once minister of finance in Malaysia, though he was ousted and jailed by the prime minister in the 90s, on accusations of sodomy.
      • When the next administration comes, he is released and the conviction overturned.
      • He goes to Oxford and Johns Hopkins to teach for a few years.
      • After a while, he goes back into politics. He focuses on anti corruption, and directs some criticism at the new prime minister. Eventually, he becomes the leader of the opposition in parliament.
      • Then, he's accused of sodomy again. and so in 2015 he is sent to prison for three years.
      • Now, he is the prime minister of Malaysia
      • progress comes and goes, and things take time
      • the only other thing I know about malaysia is nasi lemak, which is sooo delicious
  • I went to a horse race and it was just okay. It's kind of an old head activity, where you can spend your sunday afternoon sitting at the race course with a notebook doing fundamental analysis (e.g. looking for tells on the sheen of a horse's coat).
  • i am moving into a proper apartment, at last! It has been a long process of touring and logistics and I'm so relieved it's over. My sea shipment—bed, desk, lebron painting, etc.—is now on the high seas, having finally made it past the Panama Canal.
  • I miss you all! til next time
