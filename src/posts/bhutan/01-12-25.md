# January 12, 2025


Field trip! It’s sunday so there are no classes, we get on the bus to go to Phobjikha valley with the students. There are 21 boys and 13 girls so we staff members sit with the girls. I get shotgun which has a nice view. The ride there climbs almost 6000 feet of elevation—-we start at around 4000, and in order to get to the valley we need to go up through nearby mountains. The valley sits at around 9000 feet of elevation.

It’s about a 2 hour ride there, and the road is steadily increasing in elevation and twisting through the sides of mountains. The road seems cut straight out of the mountain, and it’s a wonder roads like these can even be built at all.

![E696C44D-45EA-4AE7-96CE-ABAC0CB537A4_1_105_c.jpeg](bhutan_E696C44D-45EA-4AE7-96CE-ABAC0CB537A4_1_105_c.jpeg)

Two of the girls have Aux and one of them says they’re going to play white girl party music, so it’s a lot of Katy Perry and the like. In the back of the bus a lot of them are standing up and dancing. They also play some Hindi songs, some Dzongkha songs, and some Nepali songs, and the karaoke we did with Dorji was actually helpful, since I can recognize some songs. There’s one, Am pejol (this is probably not how you spell it), where we learned the dance from Dorji, which was fun.

On the road we see yaks on the road, as well as horses. Yaks are only at high altitudes.

We arrive at a temple, and it’s a majestic four story building called the Gangteng Monastery. In front of the temple, we take pictures, and many of the students take turns taking pictures with us. All of them are in their formal dress, gho or kira. They also bring a white scarf that they wrap around their body, and Dorji says this means it is as formal as it gets. The color of this scarf holds importance, and the king will have a yellow one. One of the girls brought a kira for Serena, so she changes into it. Unfortunately Zian and I don’t have a gho, but the boys say they’ll bring me one next week. We walk around the temple, clockwise, before we go inside.

Generally, without formal wear, foreigners can’t go inside the temple, or at least not to certain places in the temple. Dorji talks to some of the people there, and they say yes I can take some paths but not others. We take our shoes off and climb up a ladder to enter the temple.

The buddha in this temple comes in 8 forms, and there are depicted in murals and in sculptures. In one room you pray for longevity, and in others you pray for auspiciousness. We have some holy water, which you drink and then pat on your head and your clothes. One section says no tourists but the kids say just go in, and so we do.

![67EF6BFC-549C-45CB-86A3-DF8AABEE0463_1_105_c.jpeg](bhutan_67EF6BFC-549C-45CB-86A3-DF8AABEE0463_1_105_c.jpeg)

After the temple, the boys get on the bus and change out of their formal clothes. The girls mostly stay in their formal clothes.

We’re getting ready to do a hike into the valley, where we’ll eat lunch together. Dorji tells us it’s about a 30 min to 1 hour walk to our lunch destination. This turns out to be hugely inaccurate, because the kids all want to stop at different places and take pictures. I’m mostly walking with a group of the older boys. As we go through the woods, they teach me things—they know which seeds they can eat and some berries that look poisonous. We stop at a stream and find a skull of a cow next to the river. The trail is often unclear and it’s very easy to get lost, so you really know the general direction you’re going. It’s a marshy area, so you really need to be careful lest you step in a big pile of mud.

The special thing about this valley is that there are black-necked cranes, who stay here over the winter months. Over the summer, they are in Tibet. For this reason, it’s a protected area, and we can see the cranes from afar. They are huge, and to see the white and black glide through the sky is incredible. For almost all of the students, this is a new place as well, and one says it’s one of the best days of his life. Another one is unimpressed. She says it looks not so different from where she’s from.

The trail takes us almost 3 hours. The boys I’m with want to take pictures, so they can use for their profile pictures. I also do some poses with Dorji. A lot of them want to use my phone for pictures since it’s an iphone. Most of them seem to have vivo phones? I ask them when they got their phones, and it’s a wide range—some just last year, and some in grade 4.

At one point in the middle of the trail I realize we have been extremely unprepared. Most of the students didn’t bring a bag or any water, and it’s a pretty long walk with some seriously steep uphill sections. The water I have gets passed around between the students, and I also give my jacket to one of the students since she’s visibly shaking. Even though it’s just around 3, the sun is going down, and here the sun gives so much warmth. The temperature difference between night and day is likely more than 60 degrees Fahrenheit. I’m in a sweater and not cold at all during the hike, but by the time we reach our lunch destination, I’m quite cold. We kept the food on the buses, and have a bit of a picnic, where we sit in a big circle. The food is delicious and I eat a lot because the spice makes me feel a bit warmer.

![23D50D03-F616-4A22-8B31-393CF3294418_1_105_c.jpeg](bhutan_23D50D03-F616-4A22-8B31-393CF3294418_1_105_c.jpeg)

Thankfully, we get back on the bus after a bit, and there is heating, and we are on our way.

The girls in the back of the bus decide it’s party time again, and at one point they are screaming let it go, haha. They bring their phone flashlights out and it’s a party floor. Some of them don’t sit down for the whole bus ride back. We pass the bus with the boys and it looks like they’re doing the same. Some of the other girls sleep though, and it does seem like the girls have divided into two distinct groups. It was especially apparent during the day as they were walking in very different groups, and they don’t really seem to talk much between groups. Serena and I talk about it a bit later, but we’re not really sure what can be done about this. Teenage social dynamics.

We get back, meet our new driver, who’s called Namgay, and he generally works for tourism in Thimphu. He seems nice but we don’t talk to him for long. We need to start grading, since we promised to give their students the exams back the next day. Grading takes a few hours, but we’ve realized our plans for the coming week might be much too hard based on the exam. It’s hard to expect students to internalize the many new concepts we’ve shown, but we also don’t have time for much review.
