# January 31, 2025

It is our last day in Bhutan. We are scheduled to visit the Royal Academy, which is a school that one of our students attends. It's apparently an important and fancy school directly funded by the government. We drive up and up, and stop at a point where we can watch airplanes take off. We eventually get to the school which is sort of nestled into the mountains. It's quite remote which means that the students, who live there, can't really leave campus.

![alt text](BC65115A-EC2C-4D46-8E25-5AC8708805AC_1_105_c.jpeg)

We talk to the principal, who is an indian man, and he briefly talks to us about our program. He mentions listening to Lex Friedman podcasts, which I find a bit funny.

We then do a campus tour. The campus is huge and simply beautiful. The main academic building looks like a dzong from the outside, so it's furnished very traditionally, but inside there are very technological classrooms. The kids are on break but we meet a few of them who are doing some sort of fablab program, in collaboration with MIT.

We meet a few of the teachers, including a guy who did startups in California but moved to Bhutan to teach for a year. Teaching here sounds like a pretty good gig. He rides a motorcycle and a ride behind him as we move between places on campus.

We get lunch in one of the cafes inside the dzong, and I order some noodles. It's the first non Bhutanese food I've had in a while, and frankly I'm not used to it, haha.

Then we have to go to the airport, and as I breathe my last few breaths of the fresh mountain air, I think about what we've done here. It's been a crazy past month, but also one of the most rewarding experiences I've had. The people I've met here, especially our students, have been earnest and gracious and genuine. I was reminded time and time again of my love for teaching, and of math, and of the tremendous joy that comes from creating community.
