# January 16, 2025


Somehow I missed this day, so I’m writing a day late. It may be because I’m finally succeeding at sleeping—I got eight hours the past two nights, which is a bit of a record.

The main notable thing that happens during the day is that people from the Royal Society for Stem come. Karma is a man who is a director for the program, and he brings in two students, who were selected for having some of the highest marks in Bhutan that year. They are applying to US colleges and apparently here as TAs to help us with the program. But we do have Tandin, and we’re not totally sure how to use them. Both of the two kids are very good at English, and it’s clear they have much of the support of the government. This Royal Society for Stem is a big deal, and to be very honest it seems like they may have somewhat larger egos because of it. They talk about coming here to help inspire the students, which is nice, but also I don’t really know how we can use them for academic purposes because neither of them have done math competitions and at this point they know less than many of our students when it comes to the material, and certainly much less than Tandin.

So all of a sudden the staff at this program goes from just the three MIT students and Dorji to having Tashi back as well as three TAs. It’s nice to have help but now it also sort of feels like we have more responsibility—instead of just teaching, we have to sort of manage staff as well, which comes with its own responsibilities.

I run the livesolving session after classes, from 5-6. The idea is that Zian and Serena would solve old IMO problems that I had picked out, and students could get a sense of how they might think and approach a new problem. The first problem is number theory, and Zian gets stuck for a while, and it ends up being quite an instructive moment. I was a bit hesitant about the event initially, but I think the students seem engaged enough. Then I give Serena a geometry problem, but this ends up being a bit of a mistake, since it’s really hard for students to understand the motivations behind complex geometry problems.

I wasn’t planning on doing a question but then the students want me to do one so I reluctantly choose one, but it ends up being an IMO 5 and a bit harder than I had initially anticipated. I mess up twice but with a little help from Zian I get it and the session ends. We told the hotel that we’d eat with the students, so we stay even afterwards. I walk with Dorji to a second canteen and we get drinks. The drink is called ara, and it’s made from maize. Tashi apparently knows how to brew it, and many families will have many stores of ara for different occasions. He teaches me how to say “delicious”—shimbe, so we say, ara shimbe! Here in Bhutan even though these liquors are quite strong, probably at least 30 to 40 percent, people don’t take shots. We just get large glasses and then sip it. We talk for a while about more cultural differences, such as how in Bhutan people don’t really share food and drinks when they order at restaurants, but other asian countries do.

Tashi comes and we talk a bit about the program. He’s really ambitious with the program, and I can tell there is some external pressure. He says last year Tandin got a 7, and this year if the students do worse then it’ll make the program look bad, and maybe funding will be harder. I tell him it’s a long term process and hard to evaluate at such an early stage. I tell him if you want students to do well, they need to be able to work outside of just this one month every year—it seems like many of these kids have never done math competitions before this camp, and likely won’t be able to do any afterwards. Some schools are really busy so there isn’t much time for outside resources, especially when they can’t bring phones or electronics. I hope at least the IMO team will have access to the internet to find resources for themselves, because otherwise it’ll be really difficult. He asks me if I’d be available for virtual training later this year, and I sort of dodge the question, telling him that really he needs to help students learn to study by themselves, and that’s part of our goal as well. We’ll give the motivated students the resources to help themselves, and after that there’s not too much we can do.

Dinner with the students is great as usual and very delicious. Our new driver hangs out at TTI and we go back and sleep soon after.
