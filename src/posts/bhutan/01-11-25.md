# January 11, 2025


Long day today! In the morning Zian holds a review session and I finish up writing my trivia. Then we have an office hours session, where the kids kind of have a chance to work on any problems and talk to us about everything. Here you see that students are maybe getting more comfortable with us—with the unstructured time, a few students are texting/watching videos/playing games, a few are really intensely working.

During lunch I talk to a group of girls and I ask them what they think of the trip tomorrow, and apparently they hadn’t heard that they were going on this trip tomorrow. It’ll be nice to have a trip with all the students, and the students seem very excited. They’re talking about what they wear on the trip and whatnot. For us, since we don’t have Bhutanese formal clothes (gho or kira), we won’t be able to enter many parts of the Dzong. We are thinking of trying to borrow from the hotel, but we haven’t really asked yet.

![0DEE4EB2-1D47-46A7-8630-58E264C88678_1_105_c.jpeg](bhutan_0DEE4EB2-1D47-46A7-8630-58E264C88678_1_105_c.jpeg)

We really didn’t know what to expect with the test going in. I’m sort of always overconfident in how the students will do, so I personally feel like it’s a bit easy, but in reality it’s likely still hard. I proctor one of the rooms and it feels weird to be the teacher proctoring the test and walking around the room looking at what the students are doing.

We try to repeatedly emphasize, both before and after the test, that test results aren’t that important, and we want them to not be too stressed about it. But it’s a hard message of course, and most students are quite motivated. I guess there’s a lot of opportunity on the line for some of them, in a way that’s hard for me to imagine—going to the IMO means a trip abroad.

I hold trivia in the session after the test, and I think it goes pretty well. I ask them to make groups that contain both boys and girls, but this is actually surprisingly difficult, since I they’re bit shy and the boys maybe aren’t really used to talking to the girls and vice verse. I actually think I made the trivia a bit too easy—they know more pop culture than I expect. I didn’t really know if people like tom holland, ariana grande, were recognized here, but apparently at least for kids they know them quite well. They are on instagram, after all. They do seem to enjoy trivia, and I’m excited to run lots of these types of activities—I think I’ll do an estimathon next week.

After trivia I go and play basketball with some of the guys. We have a nicely inflated ball and play five on five, and it’s quite intense. During the day there were some cows on the court and there’s a large cow poop, but otherwise it feels no different from an outside court I’ve seen in the US. You just have an extra fun obstacle you need to watch out for.

After basketball, we go to the dinner area, and there’s some students playing guitar. I ask them to play some songs, and they sing bhutanese songs, nepali songs, and english songs too. I find they know some english songs better than I do. I do some songs too, like perfect by ed sheeran 😀, and we talk about singing and music for a while. All of them are really musical and sing quite well—somehow the students here just really do love music.

Afterwards we eat. The students talk about how lunch is always the best, and I don’t know if it’s because we are there for lunch, but it probably is. Dinner tastes great though (i think it’s just one option instead of the two for lunch), but it’s really nice to spend more time talking with students. I ask some girls about when they sleep and they say midnight, but this morning they woke up at 4 or 5 to study for the test. I feel really bad and I tell them sleeping is important for thinking sharply, but really there’s not much we can do, I think. Everyone has crammed for tests before.

Post dinner we meet up with Dorji and Sangay, and we drive off to our planned karaoke. On the drive Sangay tells us that he’s actually going back to Thimphu, and another driver is going to be with us for the rest of our time here. It’s a bit of a bombshell, since we assumed we’d have Sangay the whole time, and I’m instantly saddened. We talk about it and apparently Sangay just found out today that his boss requested him to return to Thimphu. Apparently 2 drivers already went on leave because of this boss, and Sangay is going to go on a 21 day leave and just drive taxi in Thimphu. But for some reason they’re sending a different person who’ll stay in the hotel with us.

Karaoke is awesome and we stay for almost 3 hours. In the beginning i thought the room was filled with cigarette smoke, but it’s actually incense. We alternate between Dorji singing Bhutanese songs and then me doing English songs that they might know, such as Baby or Thinking out Loud or Someone Like You. Dorji is a really incredible tenor, and he can sing any Bhutanese song perfectly. We order some beers and also take turns with another group who’s behind us and doing Bhutanese songs. At one point the other group just really likes to hear Dorji sing, so on their turn they give the mic to Dorji and they just dance. We do a bit of dancing, and they also do some Hindi songs. Sangay tells us he knows Hindi, and apparently English is his worst language out of four, which is crazy impressive. We end with APT and go home. I give MIT mugs to Dorji and Sangay. I sort of wish I brought more things from the US.
