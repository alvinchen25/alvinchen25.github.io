# January 20, 2025

It was a long day, and a trip with the kids again.

In the morning we have breakfast fairly early, and then we drive off to the school. The boys have brought me a gho, which they dress me in. It's a complicated procedure and I don't know how someone could do it by themselves. The belt around my stomach is extremey tight, and I can feel it restricting me as I breathe. But that is how it is meant to be worn. They say I look Bhutanese. 

We hop on buses to take us to the nearby Wangdue Dzong, which is apparently the second longest dzong in Bhutan (but not the second biggest haha). We dont spend much time there because we have a lot of destinations for the day.

Underneath the gho I wear pants, which is apparently not okay for formal places like the dzong. I should have brought leggings, or knee length socks, but I have neither. It turns out the pants arent a huge deal because I have shorts that I can put on. But im also lacking a scarf, and most Bhutanese people only have one, so I need to borrow from a local canteen in order to go in. Luckily the students help with that.

But we manage to make it work and we explore many rooms. At one point we need to find a monk to help unlock a room, and after we pray he pours holy water into our hands. You drink some and pour the rest on your head. It’s hard for me to manage my scarf and it keeps on falling off, but the boys help me and I manage. We take lots of pictures at a large tree in the central courtyard in the dzong.

![0AC901DC-0575-4C8F-99C5-F2EFEB861D10_1_105_c.jpeg](0AC901DC-0575-4C8F-99C5-F2EFEB861D10_1_105_c.jpeg)

We then proceed to our second stop, which is Chimi Lakhang. It’s a fertility temple and nearby there are many stores selling wooden carved penises of all shapes and sizes. This is near the restaurant we had been to on our first full day in Bhutan, and it explains why that restaurant also had a penis sculpture.They say that at this temple, couples will come and pray for a baby. There was a demon who was said to have a penis so large it would scare away demons. Sometimes women will buy one of the penises and go up to the temple and pray with it. It’s a small hike up to the temple and inside they give us string which we tie around our necks after we pray.

Inside the temple there is a monk who talks to me in Dzongkha and the students laugh because even outside people think we look Bhutanese. Not too many foreigners come here and wear gho.

![alt text](9B402457-0112-4711-BAF0-BEE49CCDBB58_1_105_c.jpeg)

As we drive away, the bus encounters another line of cars from the other direction, and the road is simply too narrow, so after some tension where nobody is moving, the cars on the other side back up and let us through. Buses get right of way, I guess.

We stop at a field for lunch, and after lunch we spend some time at the river skipping stones. Some of the boys really know how to throw a stone, and they also know the technique to toss a stone all the way across the river. I try, and only make it about 3/4 of the way. They joke about making sure the angle is 45 degrees so the rocks can go further. They know physics!

We talk about swimming and Tandin mentions that he's actually taken a swimming lesson before, since his dad is involved in the Olympics. Most of the kids don't know how to swim, however.

After lunch we go to Punakha Dzong, which you can tell is one of the biggest tourist destinations since we see lots of foreigners. I had not seen a white person in many weeks. It’s huge and amazing and considered perhaps the most beautiful dzong in Bhutan. Inside there is a huge buddha, and the details on everything are incredibly intricate. You can tell it is a sacred and holy place. They make Zian take his shoes off because he isn't wearing proper shoes, so he needs to walk around on the cold stones with bare feet. But I don't think my shoes are proper either.

Apparently the first king is buried there, and a sacred relic is kept there too, and there only two people who can access it—-the king and the religious head. I don’t really know about the logistics of that but regardless you can tell that things are taken very seriously. There's one building where only locals are allowed, and a security guard stops us and tells the boys that I can’t go. But later I see Serena walk out of that building and I see that she has somehow passed as Bhutanese, even to the security guard. Zian passes as well so it might be because he heard us talking in English.

We spend a lot of time taking pictures at Punakha Dzong, since it's considered one of the most beautiful sights. There is a bridge where you can see the whole Dzong. We stand on the bridge and throw some of the extra rice over for the fish. When the rice hits the water we can see a crazy fish commotion.

![alt text](0FA11836-2F68-48BC-B87A-7BC636BD6DAD_1_105_c.jpeg)

After that we go to the suspension bridge, which is crowded so you sort of have to push past people to get across. We try pani puri, which is an indian snack with a kind of crispy ball filled with a flavorful water. It's a bit spicy and you eat it all in one bite. We have taught the kids some basic chinese, like nihao, and one of them says it as a Chinese tourist walks by, and she turns around and looks at us funny.
