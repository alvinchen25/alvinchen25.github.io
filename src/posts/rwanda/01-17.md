# 1/17

Created: January 17, 2024 12:39 PM

Today’s about the halfway point of the program. It has been quite a time and though I miss laundry machines and fast wifi and I hate the smell of diesel on the road when you walk or bike anywhere in the city, I feel strangely comfortable here in ways that I didn’t think I would be before. I’ve negotiated with mototaxi drivers and I can sometimes piece together bits of Kinyarwanda when they tell me their numbers. I’m comfortable around the coaches, who are all university students around my own age, and I’m comfortable enough with the food, though sometimes it is just beans and rice and beans and rice.

I’m supposed to visit LNDC today with Jessica. It’s an all girls school, and it’s had multiple IMO team members in the past few years, including one who’ll likely be on either the IMO or PAMO team this year. They’ve had a student go to MIT as well. We agreed last night that we’d meet at the school at 9:30, since that’s when we were told to arrive. We’re expecting to do a session at 11 and then another class at 4. I wake up early and leave the apartment by 9, since it’s a 20 minute moto transport to the school. This is actually my first time hailing a moto and taking it myself, and I pay 1.5k, which is on the higher end but still reasonable. On the way, I see a notification on my watch that Jessica is calling me, but I don’t pick up because I don’t want to take my phone out of my pocket and risk dropping it while on the motorbike. I get to the school just in time—it’s around 9:27 when I arrive. I don’t see Jessica, so I try to return her call, but it’s difficult since my data plan is malfunctioning. I eventually figure it out and call Jessica and she tells me that our school visit today is canceled. I tell her that I’m already at the school. She’s like wtf and says okay I’m coming, wait for me there. Theoneste calls as well and also tells me that the school visit is canceled. I ask why and he says it’s because they weren’t willing to provide us the AIMS students, who are the ones we work with.

Anyway, Yuka isn’t starting her school visit until 5 and she said she’s going to walk around and maybe go some places before then, and I tell her I’m now free and near downtown, so she should come downtown. She says she needs to do some work and wants to go to an art place, but will meet us downtown at some point. So Jessica and I don’t really know what to do and we just walk around the downtown area. We walk past a building and she says people used to jump off of it a lot. I ask her why the roof was public and is it still public, and she says maybe, so we go into the building and try to go to the top but it’s locked. We circle around the block once or twice and eventually decide to eat some brunch, and we go to a place called Simba restaurant, which is connected to the nearby Simba supermarket. We sit down and the menu is very american—pizza and burgers and the such, and hey, why not see what a Rwandan burger tastes like. I ask Jessica what she’ll order and it turns out she’s very picky. She doesn’t eat anything that swims, or anything that flies, and she doesn’t like tea or coffee, and she doesn’t like milk or cheese because they smell. I say that maybe you’d like milk in the US more because it has much less flavor than milk in Rwanda. We both order citron sodas and burgers and while we’re waiting for our food Theoneste messages me asking for my twitter, because AIMS Rwanda wants to post about us and tag us. I say I don’t remember if I have a twitter but I can make one, and I struggle to create an account because you need to put a number in, but my US number isn’t active and my Rwandan number doesn’t work. I try a few times before I randomly decide to test an old email account and password and it lets me in, so it turns out I did have a twitter. I change the email, handle, and profile picture, and send it back over to Theoneste.

Our food arrives and the burger comes with a tasty seeded bun and some sauce. The meat looks somehow different from ground beef I’ve seen in the US, but it’s delicious and the sauce that comes with it reminds me of Worcestershire sauce or Chick-fil-a sauce or something like that. Jessica says she cooks often, mainly because she’s picky so it’s easier to make food herself. She’s removing pieces of onion from her burger as she says this, so I know she’s serious.

A lot of the Rwandan coaches want to know what life is like in the US, and my go-to has been showing them pictures of when it snows, and when the river freezes, and things like that. It’s of course hard for someone who’s lived here their entire life to imagine something like a river freezing. And I tell them things have been just as shocking for me coming here, I suppose.

Jessica clearly wants to travel outside of Rwanda. I hope she’ll be able to soon.

Yuka texts us that she’s here, and we finish eating and find her. Yuka always has ideas of where to go, and we visit some site/museum which basically consists of a german guy’s house from 1907 from when he lived in Kigali. We walk there and the fees are 10k for the historical house tour and 2k for viewing reptiles so Yuka and I just go look at reptiles, and we find a crocodile and many snakes. The snakes are in cages without much space and the crocodile is outside in her cage. We walk through and look at the animals. When we come out, Claudine has arrived. Claudine is the coach that Yuka was going to do her school visit with today, but she informs us that their school visit is canceled as well, so we both have free days. Meanwhile, Tony is at a school 3 hours away—I feel a bit bad for him. Fortunately, he gets a break tomorrow while Yuka and I go to a primary school, I think.

The four of us walk to the UR campus where Jessica stays and Claudine is a student (off-campus). Many of the coaches study here. On the way we pass by the old presidents house. He moved recently, and you can tell because there aren’t security guards around the campus. Here, nobody knows what his house looked like. It’s not like the White house. You can only see the bushes that surround the outside.

We get to UR and the campus is quite nice—this is probably one of the best undergrad universities here, if not the best. It’s quite big, and walking across takes about as long as walking across MIT. There’s many dormitories and classrooms separated by lots of grass, and the students here are well dressed. Jessica leaves us to go back to her dorm, and I am tired as well from the walking. Once we exit the campus I take a motobike back home. No teaching is a bit refreshing, and it’s nice to have some time to reflect because it’s been so busy here. :). Probably one of the most chill days here! I could not imagine being a schoolteacher, because after two days I am exhausted.
